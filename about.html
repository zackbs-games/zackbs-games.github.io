About Zack

The story of a 6-year-old game designer and his AI adventure

🌟 Who is Zack?

Zack is a six-year-old kid who loves Pokémon, Minecraft, and anything to do with games. Like a lot of kids his age, he had a head full of ideas for games he wanted to play — but unlike most kids, he actually made them.

Both games on this site are real, working, 3D-graphics browser games — and the core ideas behind each one came from Zack. He decided what the games would be about, what creatures would appear, how battles would work, and what players should be able to do.

"I want to make a game where Pokémon have to fight off the humans for a change."

That one idea from Zack became Humans vs Pokémon — a full tower-defense game with eight Pokémon species, an evolution system, projectile attacks, and waves of human trainers.

🤖 How Were the Games Made?

Zack can't write code yet — but he can describe what he wants, make decisions, and give feedback. And that turns out to be exactly what it takes to direct an AI.

Using Claude (an AI assistant made by Anthropic), Zack would describe what he wanted the game to look like, how it should feel to play, and what should happen when you click or press a button. The AI would write the code, and Zack would test it, say what he liked, what felt wrong, and what needed to be different.

It's a lot like being a game director — you don't have to build every brick yourself, but you need a clear vision and the willingness to keep going until it feels right. Zack had both.

"Can you make the world bigger? And I want there to be snow at the top of the mountains."

That became part of Pixamon — a full 3D voxel world with grass, dirt, stone, water, and yes, snow-capped peaks.

🛠️ The Technology

Both games use some genuinely cutting-edge web technology. They're not simple pixel-art games — they run on WebGPU, the newest graphics API available in browsers, which normally takes experienced engineers years to learn.

The AI wrote all the low-level graphics code, shader programs, and engine logic. Zack provided the creative direction. The result is two games that push what's possible in a browser tab.

🚀 What's Next?

Zack isn't done. He has a list of things he wants to add to both games — saving your progress, more Pokémon, new biomes, multiplayer, and possibly an entirely new game he hasn't revealed yet.

If you have a favourite moment in either game, or a suggestion for what Zack should build next, this is his little corner of the internet — check back for updates!
