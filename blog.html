Stories from making games at age 6

May 2025 Game Dev

Penguin Fight — A Game for My Big Brother

My older brother is 8 and he loves penguins. So I made him a game where a penguin fights Minecraft mobs.

My brother always watches me play Minecraft and he thinks penguins are the best animal. So I thought — what if there was a penguin inside Minecraft, fighting all the mobs? That's how Penguin Fight came from.

You play as one brave penguin and waves of Minecraft mobs come at you — zombies, creepers, skeletons, and even bosses like the Warden, the Wither, and the Ender Dragon. You can punch them, throw snowballs, and you can double-jump to reach platforms. My favourite move is the blizzard wave — press Q and a giant wall of ice shoots across the whole screen and freezes every single mob at once.

The Ender Dragon was the hardest boss to make. It flies around and shoots fireballs and it has glowing purple eyes. I told the AI exactly how I wanted the wings to flap and it took a few goes to get it right.

My brother said it was really fun and that the penguin looked cool. That was the best review I could have asked for.

May 2025 Game Dev

Dino Jump — A Game for My Little Brother

My little brother is 3 and he is obsessed with dinosaurs. So I made him his very own game.

My little brother can't read yet but he knows every single dinosaur. T-Rex, Triceratops, Stegosaurus — he knows them all. So I wanted to make him a game he could actually play, with a dinosaur as the main character.

In Dino Jump you run through a prehistoric world and jump over things. There's lava pools that glow orange, tar pits that slow you down, and food to collect. You can even double-jump, which my brother thinks is hilarious because he says "dinosaurs can't jump that high!"

I made sure it wasn't too hard because he's only 3. The controls are simple — just jumping — so he can play it without help. He played it for a really long time on the first day which made me really happy.

Making a game for someone younger than you is different because you have to think about what will make them smile, not just what you think is fun. I think I got it right.

May 2025 Devlog

What It's Like to Make Games with AI

I didn't write the code. The AI wrote the code. But I still made the game — and here's how that works.

When people hear that an AI helped make these games, they sometimes think that means the AI did everything. But that's not how it works at all.

Making a game is mostly about decisions. What should the game feel like? What happens when you win? What happens when you lose? What's fun and what's boring? Those are the hard questions, and the AI can't answer them — it can only build what you describe.

I'd say something like "I want the Pokémon to shoot leaves at the humans" and the AI would build it. Then I'd test it, and if it felt wrong — too fast, too slow, leaves going the wrong way — I'd say so, and it would fix it. We went back and forth a lot.

The part I liked best was when something I asked for came out even better than I imagined. Like when I asked for a 3D world and the AI made the mountains have snow at the top. I didn't even ask for that part — it just added it because it made sense. That was a really cool surprise.

April 2025 Game Dev

Building Pixamon — A World to Explore

The idea for Pixamon came from wanting a game that was Minecraft and Pokémon at the same time.

I really like Minecraft because you can go anywhere and build things. And I really like Pokémon because of the creatures and the battles. So I asked: what if there was a game with both?

That's how Pixamon started. The world is made of voxel blocks — like Minecraft — but it has creatures called Pixamon that roam around, and you can battle them and catch them. You can also mine stuff and build things.

The hardest part to explain to the AI was how the creatures should move. I wanted them to walk around on their own and sometimes come toward you when they see you. That took a few tries to get right. But once it was working it was really fun to explore the world and suddenly have a creature pop up.

My favourite part of Pixamon is the snow mountains. They look really cool from far away.

March 2025 Game Dev

My First Game: Humans vs Pokémon

The idea came from watching too many Pokémon episodes and thinking: what if the Pokémon were the defenders?

In Pokémon, the humans are always the heroes. They catch Pokémon and make them battle each other. I thought that was a bit unfair. So I made a game where the Pokémon get to defend their home against the humans instead.

It's a tower defense game. You place Pokémon in a grid, and waves of human trainers try to cross it. Each Pokémon has a different attack — Squirtle shoots water, Charmander shoots fire, Jolteon shoots lightning. And if you collect enough berries, you can evolve them into stronger forms.

The thing I'm most proud of is the evolution system. When a Pokémon evolves it gets bigger and stronger and its attacks change. Watching Charmander evolve into Charizard during a hard wave feels really satisfying.

This was the first game I ever helped make. I didn't know if it would actually work — but it did, and you can play it right now on this website.
